## Headline

**The wall-reward reshape is implemented, tested and merge-ready. Measured against
a correctly-evaluated control it is a net regression, and nothing about the shipped
model changes.**

Those are two separate claims and the distinction is the point of this document:

- **The code** is behaviour-neutral by construction. Every new term is behind a
  default-off flag, and with the flags off both engines reproduce master's
  arithmetic exactly. That is what makes the branch safe to merge before the
  experiment concludes.
- **The experiment did not produce a better model, and on its headline metric it
  produced a worse one.** Re-evaluating all 18 control checkpoints post-fix (done
  in this session, 2026-08-04's comparison was invalid) shows `wallshape` raising
  wall contact 38% at goal-rate parity, and costing 0.062 of high-density goal --
  both statistically clear. Its one significant win is the corridor scenario it
  was designed around. `example_model/policy_r0125.onnx` remains the shipped
  artefact; **there is no new ONNX to ship.**

There is no verifiably best model among the three arms: the control wins on wall
contact and high density, `wallshape` wins on `corridor_t1` and (weakly) on
agent-agent metrics, and neither dominates. Details and numbers in "Matched-rollout
comparison" below.

## What the branch delivers

Eight commits, plus three polish commits added on 2026-08-07 before the PR.

### The reward terms (7 opt-in flags + 1 cap)

The motivating diagnosis, recorded in `docs/environment_mechanics.md` B.1: trained
policies ram walls at full speed rather than yielding, and this was **reward-optimal,
not a perception failure**. Walls had a flat penalty band (a step, not a gradient, so
no signal to brake before contact), *waiting* beside a wall was taxed -0.1/step
while *approaching* one at 2 m/s was free until contact, and the contact penalty
scaled with full speed so a parallel slide cost as much as a head-on.

| Flag | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `use_graded_wall_proximity` | False | Linear ramp `near` -> `far` over `[radius, threshold]` instead of the flat band |
| `wall_proximity_penalty_near` / `_far` | -0.2 / 0.0 | Ramp endpoints. Band-mean equals the old flat -0.1 |
| `use_velocity_weighted_wall_proximity` | False | Scale by closing speed toward the nearest wall |
| `wall_proximity_speed_floor` / `_scale` | 0.25 / 0.5 | `pen *= max(floor + scale*closing, 0)`. At floor 0.0, yielding beside a wall is free |
| `use_wall_normal_impact` | False | Weight wall contact by the velocity component INTO the wall, not full speed |
| `wall_collision_penalty_cap` | 0.0 (off) | Floors the per-step wall penalty so the weighting discounts slow contact but never amplifies fast contact |

Supporting changes: nearest-wall *directions* alongside distances
(`crowdrl_core/collision.py`, `crowdrl_torch/walls.py`), plumbed through both step
paths only when a flag consumes them.

### One behaviour change that is NOT behind a flag

`fix(eval)` 48e9103 makes `wall_contact_rate` count an agent parked at exactly one
body radius, matching the mask that actually drives the reward. **It roughly
quadruples the measured rate** (control policy at r0100: 0.0029 -> 0.0117). Scope
is eval-only -- `wall_contact_rate` is produced by `compute_episode_metrics` and
read only by the scorecard; nothing in training or the reward touches it. The
practical consequence is in "Reading the scorecard files" below.

### Companion ONNX at every checkpoint

Every checkpoint save now also writes `policy_r{rollout:04d}.onnx`, so a mid-run
policy can be handed to another process without a `reexport_onnx.py` step. This
is unconditional -- the one change in the branch that applies to every existing
config. Cost is one `torch.onnx.export` plus a `git rev-parse` per checkpoint,
and it is wrapped best-effort so an export failure cannot kill a long run.

## Merge safety: why "behaviour-neutral" is a claim and not a hope

- All 8 new keys are present at all five required sites: numpy `reward.py`, torch
  `reward.py`/`step.py`, `crowdrl_torch/types.py` (`EnvConfig` + converter),
  `train_mappo.py` (YAML read + round-trip), and a flag-ON numpy-vs-torch
  equivalence test. The reward is implemented **twice** -- numpy for eval, batched
  torch for training -- so a key that reaches only one of them is a silent no-op in
  training; every key was checked against that individually.
- Defaults are numerically identical in all three declaration sites. With the flags
  off the rewritten proximity block reduces to the old expression term for term,
  and the `if cap < 0.0` guards mean the cap clamps never execute. Checked
  empirically as well as by reading: over a randomised 40-agent batch with every
  new flag at its default, the wall contribution reproduces the pre-branch
  flat-band formula to **2e-16**, and the two engines agree to **2e-7** (float32
  noise). Running the same probe against a `master` worktree gives a bit-identical
  result, so the branch reproduces master's arithmetic including its rounding.
  (Writing such a probe: the numpy `compute_rewards` takes `preferred_speeds`
  positionally while the torch one takes it as an optional keyword. Omit it on
  the torch side and the speed-deviation term silently uses a different value,
  producing a ~5e-3 "divergence" that is entirely the harness's fault.)
- `REWARD_COMPONENT_NAMES` is unchanged (10 channels), so scorecard and plot
  schemas are stable.
- Suite: **774 passing, 5 skipped** (the 5 JuPedSim modules `importorskip` away
  without a local 2.0 build), ruff clean. Master collects 729, so the branch adds
  45 tests.

### The three polish commits (2026-08-07)

A full-diff review found no WIP leftovers and no missing plumbing, but three test
gaps where the existing tests **could not have failed** on a real defect. Each fix
was mutation-verified -- the code was broken, the new test was watched to go red,
then restored:

1. **`wall_proximity_penalty_far` was pinned to 0.0 in every value test**, where
   `t * far` vanishes. An engine that dropped the term entirely passed all of
   them. Now tested at -0.02 across three band positions.
2. **The second clause of `need_wall_directions`**
   (`use_velocity_weighted_collision and use_wall_normal_impact`) was never
   executed by any test -- precisely the silent-no-op failure mode that
   `test_step_wall_directions.py` and `TestWallDirectionsWiring` exist to catch.
   Now covered in both engines, with two agents in wall contact at the same speed
   (one head-on, one sliding parallel) so the values must differ.
3. **Flat band x closing-speed weighting had no torch twin.** The numpy side
   pinned that the weighting also applies to the legacy flat band; the torch side
   could have weighted only the ramp.

Plus: `export_policy_onnx()` in `train_mappo.py` is now the single site that
decides which dynamics travel with a policy (the two export call sites carried
verbatim copies and could drift silently), guarded by a test asserting the
exported keys are exactly `crowdrl_core.config_io.DYNAMICS_FIELDS`. And B.1 now
states the `max_impact_speed = 10 m/s` cap on closing speed, the
`wall_directions is None` fallback, and that `use_wall_normal_impact` rides on
`use_velocity_weighted_collision`.

## Footguns in the new terms

None of these are bugs -- both engines agree on all of them -- but each will
surprise someone writing a config:

- **`use_graded_wall_proximity` makes `wall_proximity_penalty` dead.** With graded
  on, the enable-check switches to `near != 0 or far != 0`. A config that
  deliberately disabled wall proximity with `wall_proximity_penalty: 0.0` and then
  turns graded on gets a -0.2/0.0 band back from the defaults.
- **`use_wall_normal_impact` alone does nothing.** It is read only inside the
  `use_velocity_weighted_collision` branch. There is no warning; it is documented
  in the `RewardConfig` docstring and now in B.1.
- **The cap also clamps the unweighted base penalty.** With
  `use_velocity_weighted_collision=False`, `wall_collision_penalty=-3.0` and
  `cap=-2.0` gives -2.0. Same as the pre-existing `collision_penalty_cap`, so it
  is consistent, but "the weighting discounts but never amplifies" does not say it.
- **Enabling only the wall weighting also changes the agent-proximity term's
  velocity source** from post-contact to pre-contact (the `pre_contact_velocities`
  gate is shared). Harmless for the dense-JPS configs, which set
  `use_velocity_weighted_collision: true` anyway, but it is a cross-term coupling.

### Matched-rollout comparison -- and a reversed conclusion

**This is the main new result of the 2026-08-07 session, and it contradicts what
was believed on 2026-08-04.**

The belief on the day, recorded in the `wallcap` config header, was that
`wallshape` had cut wall contact 15-20% against the control. That comparison put
post-fix `wallshape` numbers against **pre-fix** control numbers -- across exactly
the boundary the eval fix created, in the direction that flatters the new arm,
because the control was being under-measured ~4x. Re-running all 18 control
checkpoints post-fix at the matched 1500-step budget reverses the sign.

Control (`speeddev`) vs `wallshape`, r0050-r0950, 18 matched checkpoints, all
metrics post-fix:

| | control | wallshape | ws-better / worse | sign-test p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| goal rate | 0.802 | 0.806 | 11 / 7 | 0.48 |
| **wall contact rate** | **0.0107** | **0.0147 (+38%)** | **4 / 14** | **0.031** |
| **high-density goal** (>=60 agents) | **0.752** | **0.690 (-0.062)** | **2 / 16** | **0.0013** |
| **`corridor_t1` seed 0 goal** | **0.36** | **0.73 (+0.37)** | **12 / 1** (5 ties) | **0.0034** |
| agent collision rate | 0.0764 | 0.0546 (-29%) | 13 / 5 | 0.096 |
| stuck agent fraction | 0.390 | 0.304 (-22%) | 13 / 5 | 0.096 |
| freeze rate | 0.217 | 0.228 | 9 / 9 | 1.00 |
| wall proximity rate | 0.0641 | 0.0688 | -- | -- |

Read it as three findings:

1. **The reshape fails its own headline claim.** It exists to reduce wall
   contact. Measured correctly it *raises* wall contact by 38%, consistently
   (worse at 14 of 18 checkpoints). That is not a subtle effect and it is not
   noise.
2. **It does deliver on the scenario it was designed around.** `corridor_t1`
   seed 0 -- the dense corridor where policies ram the wall -- goes from solved
   at 0.36 to solved at 0.73, better at 12 checkpoints and worse at 1. The
   mechanism is visible at r0450, where `wallshape` halves wall contact in that
   scenario (0.047 -> 0.027) while nearly doubling its freeze rate (0.25 ->
   0.46): agents yield beside the wall instead of pushing through, which is
   exactly what floor 0.0 was for.
3. **It costs the thing the project most needs.** High-density goal falls 0.062,
   worse at 16 of 18 checkpoints -- the strongest signal in the table, and it is
   against the known weak spot.

Goal rate is indistinguishable (+0.004, far below the project's own |dGR| > 0.055
noise floor from `plan/archive/2026-04-11_stuck_agent_tuning_summary.md`). The
agent-collision and stuck improvements are suggestive at p ~ 0.1 but do not clear
significance on 18 samples.

The plausible mechanism for finding 1: making wall-adjacent waiting free removed
the tax on *being* near a wall without adding a comparable tax on *touching* one
at low speed, so agents park against walls. `wall_proximity_rate` rises with it
(0.064 -> 0.069). This is the "wall-hugging degeneration" the `wallshape` config
header anticipated -- it just predicted a >3-4x explosion as the alarm threshold,
and what happened instead is a modest, statistically clear drift that the
on-the-day reading missed.

### `wallshape` also destabilised past r0600

Independent of the above: overall goal ran 0.77-0.89 through r0600 and 0.69-0.81
after, and high-density goal 0.69-0.84 then 0.48-0.64. The diagnosis in the
`wallcap` config header: the weighted wall-contact term at base -2.0 was
**uncapped**, so it amplified above base at speed (-2.5/step at 2 m/s), and dense
pileups shoving agents into walls charged `wall_c` -21..-39 per episode for
contact the agent does not control. That is the same amplification pathology the
agent side hit and fixed with `collision_penalty_cap`. `wall_collision_penalty_cap`
is the wall-side twin, and `wallcap` is the arm that tests it.

`wallcap` was stopped 30 minutes in at r0350, with scorecards only to r0200. Its
numbers there (goal 0.71-0.75, wall contact 0.008-0.046, high-density goal
0.49-0.62) are *below* the r0450 checkpoint it started from, i.e. it decayed
immediately rather than recovering -- the same signature as wallshape's own late
decay, which is what points at the optimisation regime rather than the cap. Too
short to be a verdict on the cap either way.

### Reading the scorecard files

Two traps, both from the eval fix landing mid-experiment:

1. **Pre-fix vs post-fix, and only for wall metrics.** Every `scorecard_rXXXX.json`
   in the speeddev directory was produced *before* 48e9103 and undercounts
   `wall_contact_rate` by ~4x; the `_fixedmetric` files are post-fix, and all
   `wallshape` / `wallcap` scorecards are post-fix throughout. **Never compare a
   wall metric across that boundary** -- but the other metrics are genuinely
   unaffected, which is worth knowing because it makes the pre-fix files still
   useful. At r0100 the inline and re-evaluated scorecards agree to 1e-12 on all
   eleven non-wall metrics (goal, freeze, stuck, collision, speed, path
   efficiency, episode length), differing only in `wall_contact_rate`. So the
   speeddev arm's goal-rate history over its full r0050-r2350 range is directly
   comparable to the wall arms; only its wall numbers needed re-running.
2. **The step budget must match.** All three arms' in-run scorecards were produced
   at `--max-steps 1500` (`scorecard_max_steps` in each config, passed by the
   training loop). Re-evaluating without that flag falls back to `max_steps: 3000`
   and *every* metric shifts, not just the wall ones -- at r0050, goal 0.888 ->
   0.926 and stuck 0.392 -> 0.011. This session hit exactly that and had to
   discard and redo a batch. The r0100 anchor is the check: at 1500 steps a
   re-eval reproduces the inline scorecard on every metric except
   `wall_contact_rate`.

## Where to take this next

The re-eval turns a vague "the arm was promising but unstable" into a specific
question: **the terms help exactly where they were aimed (the corridor) and hurt
in the aggregate.** Three readings, in the order worth testing:

1. **Re-run the cap arm with a fine-tune learning rate -- not the same recipe
   deeper.** Every number above is from the *uncapped* arm, so
   `wall_collision_penalty_cap` remains effectively untested on the aggregate
   metrics. But simply resuming `wallcap` is the wrong move: its four scorecards
   (goal 0.71-0.75, high-density goal 0.49-0.62) show it decaying **immediately**
   from the wallshape r0450 it warm-started from, which is the same signature as
   wallshape's own late decay. The likely cause is the optimisation regime rather
   than the reward shape -- `--init_from` deliberately discards optimizer,
   curriculum and history (`train_mappo.py`, "Optimizer state is also reset"), so
   an already-specialised policy is hit with a fresh LR of 5e-4 on a cosine
   schedule that has barely decayed in its first ~1000 rollouts, and the
   curriculum restarts from the beginning. The next arm should start from
   `results_exp_dense_jps_wallshape/checkpoint_rollout_0450.pt` (not the degraded
   `wallcap` r0350), keep the cap, and drop to ~1e-4 or 5e-5 or shorten the
   cosine horizon. Note `--resume_training` is unavailable here: no `history.json`
   is written mid-run, so a killed run can only be continued via `--init_from`,
   which is exactly what forces the optimizer reset.
2. **`wall_proximity_speed_floor = 0.0` may be too generous.** It makes *all*
   stationary wall contact free, not just yielding. A small non-zero floor keeps
   the yield affordance while restoring a standing cost. This is a one-line
   config change, and the floor is already plumbed.
3. **The high-density regression may be the same trade seen elsewhere.** Yielding
   near walls costs throughput exactly where throughput is scarcest. If so, the
   wall terms are worth keeping only for corridor-like geometry, which argues for
   per-tier reward weighting rather than a global flag.

Whatever runs next should evaluate **against a post-fix control**. The single
most expensive mistake in this experiment was not a modelling choice; it was
comparing across an eval-metric change.
